# Brahman ॐ

**Brahman** is a Sanatan Dharma purohit (priest) app. Tell it the puja or hawan you want to perform and it tells you
what you need, walks you through every step and recites the mantras; give it your birth details and it makes your
kundli and reads it — like a family pandit, entirely offline.

## Features

Brahman works **entirely from its built-in knowledge** — no account, no API key and no internet connection to an AI
service is needed. Ask it the way you would ask your pandit-ji:

hawan/havan, pooja/puja, laxmi/lakshmi, kundali/kundli…), and Indian date and time styles ("3-8-1995, shaam 7 baje").

### Brahman's voice

- **Natural human-like voice (optional, server).** Set `AZURE_SPEECH_KEY` + `AZURE_SPEECH_REGION` (Azure neural voices `hi-IN-MadhurNeural` and `en-IN-PrabhatNeural`, Indian male; the free tier covers 0.5M characters a month) or `GOOGLE_TTS_API_KEY` (`hi-IN-Neural2-B`). `/api/tts` synthesises sentence by sentence with a calm, slightly slower pandit delivery and pauses at each danda, caches repeats, and has per-minute and daily (`TTS_DAILY_CHAR_LIMIT`) guards. Users never need a key; without one, or if the service fails, the device voice is used.
- **Recorded mantras.** With a voice key set, `npm run build:voice` records every mantra the app recites (about 140, ~10k characters — well inside the free tier) once, as a slow, deep chant, into `public/voice/`. Recordings are played first everywhere, and `npm run build:beta` embeds them, so mantras in hawans and kriyas are recited in the human-like male voice even in the beta and offline, with no key at runtime.
- **Offline male voice (no key).** `npm --prefix tools/voice install && npm run build:voice:offline -- --kathas` records every mantra and every Hindi katha telling with the open-source Kokoro-82M model's Hindi male voice (`hm_omega`), fully offline (eSpeak NG turns Devanagari into Hindi IPA at build time). The recordings in `public/voice/` (about 55 MB: 139 mantras, plus one file per katha and per chapter) are committed, so the app and the beta speak mantras and kathas in a human-like male voice on any device with no service at all. See `tools/voice/NOTICE.md` for licences.
- **Device voice.** Otherwise the best installed Hindi male voice is chosen (Edge's "Madhur Online (Natural)" first). Listeners can pick any installed voice and hear a sample under **My details**.
- **Kathas in Hindi.** Every katha and granth is told in Hindi as a kathavachak tells it — jaikara, the story with feeling and dialogue, the teachings and the phala — with a हिंदी / English switch (Hindi by default).

- **Speak to Brahman.** Tap 🎙️ and ask in Hindi, Hinglish or English. Hindi heard in Devanagari is understood too, because the finder transliterates it ("ग्रह शांति हवन" → "grah shanti havan").
- **Brahman speaks back** when "Brahman's voice" is on, and it turns on by itself when you speak. It uses a calm, slower, deeper delivery and prefers Indian male voices where the device has them. Sanskrit and Hindi are read by a Hindi voice, English by an Indian-English voice.
- **Narrated rituals.** In "Perform with me", each step is read out and then its mantra is recited. Every answer also has a 🔊 Listen button.
- **No server needed.** All of this uses the browser's built-in speech engine. Voice quality depends on the voices installed on the device.

### Knowledge that grows

If the server has an `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, questions the built-in granth cannot answer are **searched with AI and added to Brahman's knowledge**:

1. The answer is generated in the same structured form as the built-in rites: samagri, steps and mantras for a vidhi, or a written article for other questions.
2. It is checked and saved to a shared JSON store on the server (`KNOWLEDGE_FILE`, default `data/learned-knowledge.json`).
3. From then on, every user who asks something similar gets it instantly from Brahman's own knowledge, with no AI call. Learned entries are marked ✨.

To keep this under control:
- **Review:** `/knowledge` lists everything learned. Removing an entry needs `BRAHMAN_ADMIN_TOKEN`.
- **Cost guards:** 10 searches per hour per visitor, and `LEARN_DAILY_LIMIT` (default 200) per day across all users.
- **Safety:** personal details are never stored, and off-topic or harmful questions are declined.
- **Hosting:** host on a server with a persistent disk, or point `KNOWLEDGE_FILE` at one. Serverless filesystems are wiped between deploys.

Users never need a key. Without one, the app works fully from its built-in knowledge, just without learning.

## Beta (0.1.0-beta.5)

`npm run build:beta` bundles the browser side of the app into one self-contained page (`dist-beta/index.html`, about 1.7 MB). It includes:
- the finder, puja and hawan guides with personal sankalpa and substitutes for abroad
- the katha library, kundli with four systems, predictions and remedies, Kundli Milan, panchang with muhurta finder, and prayers
- Brahman's voice

It needs no server. The beta leaves out three things that need the server or a microphone: AI search and learned knowledge, the conversational AI kundli reading, and speech input. Deep links use plain hash tokens, e.g. `#kriyas.navagraha-shanti` or `#katha.dhruva`.

## Getting started

Requires Node.js 20+.

```bash
npm install
npm run dev                  # http://localhost:3000
# optional: cp .env.example .env.local and set ANTHROPIC_API_KEY so new questions are searched and learned
```

## Notes on accuracy

- Places come from GeoNames (CC BY 4.0) with time zones from city-timezones; populations are GeoNames' own and occasionally wrong, so results always show the state and country.
- Placidus cusps are checked against Raphael's Table of Houses (London, 0h sidereal time); the KP ayanamsa follows Krishnamurti's 22°22′03″ at 1900 with 50.2388″/year.
- The ayanamsa is a linear Lahiri approximation (well under an arcminute for 1900–2100); the lunar nodes are mean nodes.
- Panchang elements are taken at local sunrise. Published panchangas differ slightly by tradition (drik vs.
  Surya Siddhanta, amanta vs. purnimanta, vrata-day rules), so the app tells users to follow their sampradaya for vrata dates.
- The kundli reading applies classical Parashari rules mechanically; it describes tendencies and recommends gentle
  remedies (japa, daan, worship), never fear-based predictions.
- Every mantra was independently audited against standard published readings (Gita Press, critical editions). No verse
  text or verse number errors were found; translation and citation refinements from the audit are applied.
- Long granths are given as chapter summaries with selected verses rather than full text, to avoid misquoting
  scripture; full texts are included only where they are short and well established (Hanuman Chalisa).
- Predictions apply textbook rules (functional benefics/malefics by lagna, dignity, combustion, aspects, Vimshottari
  dasha, Jupiter–Saturn double transit, gochar from the Moon). They are deliberately worded as tendencies with remedies.
- Lal Kitab effects and remedies follow commonly published tables (pakka ghar, aspects, debts and sleeping houses verified
  against several sources; individual remedies are not all checked one by one) — the app says to confirm major remedies
  with a practitioner. The chart is always computed with Lahiri ayanamsa (KP uses the Krishnamurti ayanamsa).
- Birth-time rectification scores Vimshottari dasha links and the Jupiter–Saturn double transit for each event; it is a
  guide that needs 3–5 well-dated events, not a certainty.
- Kundli Milan follows the most widely published North Indian tables. Gana and Vashya vary between traditions (the
  choice is documented in `lib/milan.ts`); Nadi and Bhakoot exceptions are reported without restoring points.
- Muhurtas use common Muhurta Chintamani rules; marriage and griha pravesh muhurtas should still be confirmed by a
  pandit against the lagna at the chosen moment and the family's charts.
- Mantras are given as traditionally recited; rites follow common North Indian household practice and note where
  regional customs differ or a purohit is traditionally needed.
